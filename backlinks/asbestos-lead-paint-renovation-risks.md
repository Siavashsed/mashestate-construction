## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What are the most dangerous hidden hazards homeowners encounter when renovating older houses?

The two biggest culprits in homes built before 1980 are asbestos and lead paint, and the danger comes from timing—not just their presence.

Asbestos was widely used in insulation, drywall joint compound, roofing materials, and ceiling texture through the 1970s. It's relatively benign sitting undisturbed in your walls. But the moment you sand, cut, or scrape it during renovation, you release fibers into the air. Those fibers are what cause mesothelioma, asbestosis, and lung cancer—sometimes 20-30 years later.

Lead paint follows a similar pattern. It's stable on walls, but sanding, stripping, or demolition creates lead dust. Children are especially vulnerable, and even low-level exposure affects neurological development. Adults can develop kidney damage and hypertension from prolonged exposure.

What makes these genuinely dangerous is that most homeowners don't test before they start demolition. I've seen people hire contractors who also weren't aware of what they were dealing with. One contractor I know was stripping trim from a 1975 colonial and ended up in the ER with acute lead poisoning.

The fix is simple but non-negotiable: test the materials first. Get professional abatement if needed. The cost is maybe 5-10% of your renovation budget, but it prevents permanent health damage.
