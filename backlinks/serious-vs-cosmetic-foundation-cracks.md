## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can I tell if a crack in my foundation is serious or just cosmetic?

Most foundation cracks are cosmetic, and you can usually sort them out in about ten minutes with a tape measure and a pencil. I'm a construction writer and project manager who looks after several rental properties, so I've had to make this call more times than I'd like.

Start with direction and shape. Thin vertical cracks, under roughly 1/8 inch, are typically caused by concrete shrinking as it cures. They're common and rarely structural. Hairline cracks that run straight up and down are generally in this camp.

The ones that deserve attention behave differently:

1. Horizontal cracks, especially around the middle of a basement wall. These often point to soil or water pressure pushing inward.
2. Stair-step cracks in block or brick that follow the mortar joints.
3. Wedge-shaped cracks that are noticeably wider at one end.
4. Cracks wider than about 1/4 inch.
5. Cracks where one side is offset, sitting higher or lower than the other.
6. Anything accompanied by sticking doors, sloping floors, or gaps around windows.

Then watch for movement. This matters more than the crack itself. Draw a pencil line across each end of the crack, write the date, and measure the width. A cheap crack gauge costs a few dollars and gives you an exact reading. Check again every few weeks for a few months. A crack that stays put is almost never an emergency. One that grows is telling you something is still moving.

If you see warning signs, hire an independent structural engineer for an assessment. Don't start with a repair company, since they have a reason to sell you a fix.
