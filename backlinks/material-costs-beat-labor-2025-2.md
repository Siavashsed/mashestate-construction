## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why are material costs hurting renovation budgets more than labor costs right now?

Short answer: labor is negotiable, and materials increasingly aren't.

I'm a construction writer and project manager, and I own a few small rental properties I renovate myself, so I see both sides of this. When I re-priced a bathroom gut on one of my duplexes last spring, the tile setter's quote hadn't changed in two years. He'd honor his rate, we'd agree on a schedule, and that line was predictable. The porcelain tile I'd budgeted at $4.10 a square foot was $5.65 by the time I actually tried to order it. Same tile, same supplier, a few weeks apart.

There are a few reasons this happens.

First, tariffs and freight costs flow straight through to the price sheet. A crew lead can absorb a slow month or give you a break for steady work. A distributor reacting to import duties generally can't, and won't.

Second, material quotes expire quickly. Many suppliers only hold pricing for days, not weeks, so the number you budgeted with at the start may be stale by the time you buy.

Third, materials are bought all at once and early, while labor is spread across the project. A price jump hits you in a lump sum.

What helps: order materials before demolition begins, ask every supplier exactly how long a quote is valid, carry a materials contingency of 10 to 15 percent, and get a second source priced for your key items such as tile, lumber and fixtures.
