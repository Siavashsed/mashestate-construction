**Title:** Why does every contractor quote 6 weeks when the whole renovation really takes 4 to 6 months?

I manage renovation projects and own a few rentals, and the gap between the schedule people hear and the schedule they live through is the most common source of homeowner frustration I see.

When a contractor says six weeks, they're usually counting the part they control: the on-site work. In March 2022 I took on a 1960s ranch kitchen for a rental outside Columbus. My crew was on site for 5 weeks and 2 days. The whole job, from first sketch to final punch list, took far longer.

Most of that time isn't hammer time. It's design decisions, permit review, ordering long-lead items like cabinets and windows, waiting for inspections, and the punch list at the end where small fixes drag on. None of those are the contractor lying. They're just outside the number he quoted.

What helped me most was asking every contractor one question up front: 'Does your timeline start at the signed contract or at the first day of demo?' It reframes the whole conversation.

For anyone planning a kitchen or bath, I'd budget four to six months and treat anything faster as a bonus. Curious what timeline surprises others have run into.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How long does a kitchen renovation really take from start to finish?

Realistically, plan on four to six months for a whole-room renovation like a kitchen, even if a contractor tells you six weeks. Both numbers can be honest, because they measure different things.

The six-week figure usually covers only the on-site construction: demolition, rough-in work, installation, finishing. That's the part the contractor controls directly. The rest of the timeline lives outside the crew's schedule.

Here's how the full project typically breaks down:

1. Design and planning. Layout decisions, selecting finishes, and finalizing a scope take longer than most people expect, especially if you're comparing options or waiting on a designer.

2. Permits. Depending on your municipality, review can take anywhere from days to several weeks, and a revision request restarts the clock.

3. Ordering materials. Custom or semi-custom cabinets, windows and specialty appliances often have lead times of 8 to 12 weeks or more. Demo shouldn't start until the critical items are confirmed.

4. Construction. This is the six-week part. On a 1960s ranch kitchen I handled for a rental outside Columbus in March 2022, my crew's on-site work took 5 weeks and 2 days.

5. Inspections and punch list. Inspections need scheduling, and small corrections, a missing trim piece, a paint touch-up, a cabinet door that needs adjusting, can stretch for weeks.

My practical advice: ask any contractor whether their timeline starts at the signed contract or at demo day, and get the answer in writing. Order long-lead items before demolition. Build a buffer of at least 20 percent into your expectations, and avoid scheduling events like holidays around the finish date.
